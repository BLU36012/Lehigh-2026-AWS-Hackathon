      How to read this — why the same scan costs different amounts at the same hospital

      A hospital does not have one price. It has a separate negotiated price with every insurer, and often a different one for each plan that insurer sells. A lumbar MRI at Lehigh Valley Hospital runs from $212 on one Cigna plan to $3,049 on an Independence Blue Cross PPO. Same scanner, same radiologist, same building. The only thing that changed is the card in your wallet.

      Negotiated rate also: allowed amount
      The total the hospital agrees to accept from you and your insurer combined. It is the number this page compares. It is not your bill.
      Gross charge also: chargemaster, list price
      The hospital's sticker price before any discount. Almost nobody pays it. It mainly matters to the uninsured and to out-of-network billing.
      Discounted cash price
      What the hospital accepts from someone paying without insurance. Sometimes lower than the insured rate, which is why it is worth asking for either way.
      Deductible
      What you pay yourself before the insurer starts contributing. While it is unmet, you pay the full negotiated rate — so the rate is entirely your problem, not theirs.
      Coinsurance
      Your percentage share after the deductible is met. 20% coinsurance on a $1,000 allowed amount is $200.
      Copay
      A flat fee for a visit type, charged instead of deductible-and-coinsurance maths. Common for office and urgent care visits.
      Out-of-pocket maximum
      The yearly ceiling on your own spending, after which the plan pays everything. Traditional Medicare Part B has none.
      Facility fee vs professional fee
      The facility fee buys the room, equipment and nursing; it is what these files publish. The clinician who treats you bills separately. Two bills for one visit is normal, not an error.
      CPT / HCPCS code
      The five-character code identifying the exact service. Get it in writing before the procedure — a different code is a different price.
      MS-DRG
      Used for inpatient stays. One bundled payment covers the whole admission rather than itemising each day, so a DRG price is not comparable to an outpatient line item.
